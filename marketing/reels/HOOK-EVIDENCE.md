# Why the hook is built this way: evidence

This maps each element of the "max hook" (versions D and E of `2026-11-20-types-of-students`) to published research or platform data. Where the evidence is weak or mixed, it says so.

## 1. Why the first 2–3 seconds decide everything

| Finding | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **47% of a mobile feed video ad's value is delivered in the first 3 seconds, and 74% in the first 10** (Facebook research with Nielsen). | [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-offers-tips-for-converting-tv-ads-to-mobile/448510/), [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/digital-tv/facebook-adds-viewable-video-options-automated-captions-autoplay/), [Martech](https://martech.org/even-brief-video-views-drive-brand-lift-facebook-nielsen-study-finds) | Strong, but dated (about 2016–17) and about ads |
| TikTok's Creative Codes treat the first **6 seconds** as the hook stage; secondary sources attribute to TikTok Marketing Science a **swipe-or-stay decision in about 1.7 s**. | [TikTok Creative Codes (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf); 1.7 s figure via [Coinis](https://coinis.com/how-to/first-3-seconds-tiktok-video-ad) | Official framework; the 1.7 s figure is unverified |
| Meta: Reels hooks land "within the first few seconds", the moment viewers decide whether it's worth watching. | [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182) | Platform guidance, reported secondhand |
| YouTube Shorts shows "viewed vs. swiped away" as its own metric: the first second is measured separately. | [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide) | Platform metric |

**So:** the hook must work in about 1.5–2 s, and the reel must keep giving reasons to stay until at least 6 s.

## 2. Each element of the hook

| Element | What we do | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People and faces, framed tight, from frame 0** | An extreme close-up of the character's face, filling the frame | Google/Kantar's ABCDs (11,000 ads): "jump in", "tight framing", and "if your video features people, open with them on-screen". Following the ABCDs was linked to up to **+30% short-term sales likelihood**. | [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/), [ABCD guide (PDF)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf), [Google Ads help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551) | Strong for real people; our faces are cartoon characters, which carry expression but aren't covered by the study |
| **The expression** | Mochi: shocked or surprised. Packy: a (forced) grin | In visual search, **happy, surprised and angry faces are found faster**; fear is inconsistent. | [Calvo & Nummenmaa 2008](https://emotion.utu.fi/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Calvo_Nummenmaa_JEPGen_08.pdf), [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.840746/pdf) | Mixed but supportive: surprise and happiness are the right picks; avoid relying on fear |
| **Abrupt onsets** | The text pops in at 0 s, the stamp slams in at ~1.3 s, the new line appears at 1.6 s | **New objects appearing suddenly capture attention automatically**; changes in colour or brightness alone don't. | [Yantis & Jonides 1984](https://researchgate.net/profile/Steven-Yantis/publication/16958392_Abrupt_visual_onsets_and_selective_attention_Evidence_from_visual_search/links/0046351b7673aea58b000000/Abrupt-visual-onsets-and-selective-attention-Evidence-from-visual-search.pdf); colour/luminance result in [Jonides & Yantis 1988](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03208805.pdf); limits in [Yantis & Jonides 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2137514/) | Strong (classic lab finding). **Lesson:** the red glow is a mood cue, not the attention-grabber; the new things appearing are |
| **Sound plus on-screen text** | An impact at frame 0 and a second at ~1.3 s, with 3–5 words in huge type | Meta: pairing sound with on-screen text made Reels ads **1.8x more likely to rank in the top 20% for brand interest**; Google's ABCDs list audio among the elements that matter most in the opening. | [Best Media Info, on Meta's Reels playbook](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/want-reels-ads-that-actually-work-meta-just-revealed-the-playbook-10866888), [ABCD guide (PDF)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf) | Moderate (ad studies, reported by press) |
| **High contrast** | The red edge glow, a bright stamp, outlined text | Google's ABCDs: keep visuals **bright and high-contrast** and avoid competing elements. | [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/) | Moderate |
| **A specific open loop** | At 1.6 s, "Every friend group has all 4. #4 is the worst 👇" | **Information-gap theory:** curiosity is the felt need to close a specific gap in what we know, and it's strongest when the gap is specific and feels closeable. "Which one is #4?" is answered only by watching to the end. | [Loewenstein 1994, via Golman & Loewenstein (CMU)](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf), [Nautilus](https://nautil.us/curiosity-depends-on-what-you-already-know-235803) | Strong theory; a classic of behavioural economics |
| **Surprise and emotion as the hook's job** | A shock reveal, then a comedic contrast as the music turns playful | TikTok's Creative Codes name **suspense, surprise and emotion** as hook techniques. | [TikTok Creative Codes (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf), summarised by [Coinis](https://coinis.com/how-to/create-tiktok-ad-hook) | Platform framework |
| **The relatable situation as the topic** | "The final is cumulative?!" is a moment every student knows | The top student-niche reels this month were relatable exam-stress posts (vidIQ outliers, Nov 2026; e.g. 847K views for "all the midterms line up the exact same week"). | `.claude/skills/studyboard-reels/references/retention.md` ("Live outlier patterns") | Strong for this audience, this season |
| **Brand present early, but not as an ad** | The companions (Studyboard's own characters) are on screen from frame 0; the name only appears at the end | Google's ABCDs recommend **showing the brand early**; our characters are brand assets, so the brand is present without a logo-first opening, which creators say reads as an ad. | [ABCD guide (PDF)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf) | Moderate |

## 3. Honest caveats

- Most of the hard numbers come from **ad** research (Facebook/Nielsen, Google/Kantar, Meta, TikTok). The mechanisms carry over to organic Reels, but the exact percentages may not.
- Several widely shared "first 3 seconds" statistics (63%, 65%, 71%) have **no verifiable primary source**, so they aren't used here.
- The real test is our own data: compare each version's **skip rate** (or "viewed vs. swiped") and **3-second hold** with the original post's. One reel is a hint; a few with the same result is evidence.
