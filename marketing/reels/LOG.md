## What we've learned

- (Add one line each time the numbers show something, e.g. "POV hooks held 2x better than question hooks.")
- Pre-flight (five lenses) on the crunch week reel caught two gaps before posting: no on-screen comment prompt, and a payoff too small to notice. Both fixed.
- vidIQ outliers (Nov 2026): the biggest student-niche hits were relatable text-over-static reels, not demos, and small accounts broke out. Testing that format alongside the fix reels.
- Quality pass (Nov 15): rebuilt the relatable reels on the top outlier's measured structure (real person at frame 0, persistent native caption, cut at ~2 s, drop on a cut at ~4 s, loop), and gave every reel owned music and sound effects (they were silent).
- Relatable reels need a product payoff for a business account (spotted by Connor): added a 3.4 s fix-and-end tail to all three, keeping the native opening.
- Pacing and polish (Connor's review): no hard cuts or flashes (cross-dissolves), more reading time (17 s structure, payoff cards held 3 s or more), sentence case, and the real Studyboard wordmark on every end card.
- Branding and layout (Connor's review): logo lockup = app icon above the official wordmark; everything centred on the frame (calendars centred on the day squares); a little more energy (yellow key words, overshoot, a small confetti burst and ding at the payoff, companions on the end card).
- Polish (Connor's review): fixed flicker (a 12 Hz shake aliasing at 30 fps) and the end-card logo flash; captions now arrive with what they describe; reaction shots get 3 s; logo lockup C (app icon + Studyboard in Lexend). `check_glitches.py` now runs on every render.
- Calendar fixes now redistribute visibly (Connor's review): Week 11's load moves into Weeks 10 and 9 in sequence, conserving the total, with rings and pops as each week receives it.
- Copy pass (Connor's review): one yellow highlight per caption on the punchline, twist or benefit; exclamation marks on turns, payoffs and wins ("Way better!").
- Added an entertainment-first set (Connor: less like ads, genuinely relatable): no pitch in the video, a small sign-off, brand in the caption. Compare their shares and follows with the feature reels.
- **First real data (Nov 15, ~2 h in):** the entertainment reel had 122 plays and 6 likes (~5% like rate), versus 54 plays and 0 likes for the feature-ad reel after ~1 day. Directional only (n = 1 each, different times), but it supports leading with relatable or entertainment content and keeping product demos as the payoff. The account has 3 followers, so the plays are mostly non-followers. Watch time and skip rate are still to come.
- **Hook lesson (Connor, from Instagram's insights on the first post):** the title-card opener ("A field guide / Every friend group…") didn't hold attention. Nothing happens and no situation lands until 2.7 s. Fix: open cold on the funniest character moment, with the situation already on screen and the hook line in the eye-line (centre, where the title will be), then hand off cleanly to the content. Versions B and C test two hook styles (callout vs. POV).
- Connor: B/C hooks still not grabbing enough in the first 2–3 s. D/E add an extreme emotional close-up, a red urgency glow, impacts at 0 s and ~1.3 s, a snap-out reveal with a slammed stamp, and an open loop at 1.6 s. Recipe saved in `retention.md`.
- **Trial Reels need a follower minimum** the account doesn't meet yet (Instagram rejected D's trial: "does not meet the trial reel follower requirement"). Until it grows, hook tests run as regular Reels on separate days and are compared on skip rate and 3-second hold.
- **D didn't fix it (Nov 18: 122 plays, 3 likes, the same plateau as v1).** The hook wasn't the only problem; the body loses people. Frame review: (1) **hook–body mismatch**: the hook promises a panic story, then at 2.5 s it turns into a listicle; (2) **energy cliff**: a full-frame close-up becomes a small slide with ~60% of the frame empty, small text, and the jokes in grey subtext; (3) **predictable**: four identical layouts, so by slide 2 there's nothing left to wait for, and "#4 is the worst" never pays off; (4) **mascots, not people**: viewers don't know Hoot or Mochi, so there's no "that's me" or "that's my friend". Rules going forward: the body must be at least as big and loud as the hook, content fills the frame, every beat escalates, the open loop pays off on screen, and relatable reels star people (real footage), with the companions as a sign-off.
- **D's insights (Nov 18):** average watch 5 s of 17.3 s, and views flat after hour 1. Viewers get through the 2.5 s hook and leave during the first slides, so the body, not the hook, is what failed. Sends (3 of 107 viewers, ~3%) are the one good signal: the *idea* is sendable, but the execution doesn't hold.
- **Connor: the effects-heavy short reels "feel like an ad".** The format outliers (Sep to Nov 2026) were one static shot or a real screen, with one static lowercase text block and music only, no sound effects. Switched the default to the native format; the screen recordings now show the real app.
- **First native post (Nov 18, ~1 day): 52 viewers, 94 views, 4 s average watch (53% of 7.5 s, vs D's 29%), 0 organic likes, comments, shares or follows.** The format held a larger *share* of the reel, and people rewatched (1.8 views per viewer), so the native look works. But: (1) 4 s is about how long the 14-word hook takes to read, so viewers read it, glanced at small app text and left before the payoff (79.9%) scrolled into view at about 3 s; (2) a product demo gives nothing to react to or send, which explains zero real engagement; (3) 52 viewers is Instagram's smallest test pool for a ~3-follower account, so one post can't settle anything. Fixes now in the native reels: hooks of 7–10 words, the payoff readable at frame 0 (the app recorded at a 360 px viewport, so its UI is about 20% bigger), and 6 s total. Next levers: post daily for two weeks (n, not polish), pick a trending sound in the app, and ideally Connor's hand or face on camera.
- **Set 3 (Nov 19):** no stock people (Connor); everything animated or the real app; something lands at frame 0 with new onsets until about 2 s; every reel has a built-in reason to comment or send; the product appears inside the story.
- **Set 4 (Nov 19):** Connor: the hook must grab *and hold*. Each reel now has a visible open loop (countdown, guess, wheel, typing suspense, escalation) with a payoff in the last ~2 s, and a different track or section for each.
- **Cross-posting (Nov 20):** TikTok ("Studyboard: Student Planner") and YouTube are connected to Metricool. Reel D ("The final is cumulative?!", 128 views on Instagram) is scheduled as a YouTube Short (Fri 3:50 PM PT, Metricool 436062195) and a TikTok (Fri 5:50 PM PT, Metricool 436062354), each at its own network's peak, with the same caption.
- **Skip-rate data (Metricool, Nov 21), the clearest signal so far.** Reach is about 100 for every reel (Instagram's first test pool for a ~3-follower account), so **skip rate is the number to move**. By first-frame type: chat screen (September) 91.7%; feature ad 84.6%; app screen recording 73.8%; companions field guide 65.8%; Mochi face close-up (D) 60.7%; **big spinning wheel 56.6%** (best). Average watch: 2.6–5.8 s. Frames that look like *text to read* get swiped; *a big object already moving* or *a big expressive face* holds. Set 5 (slot machine, Plinko, falling blocks, claw machine) is built on that: game-like motion fills the frame from frame 0, an outcome to wait for, almost no reading.
