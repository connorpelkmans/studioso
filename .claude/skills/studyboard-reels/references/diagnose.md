# Five lenses: diagnose and pre-check

Each weak metric points to a different cause, so each has its own lens. Use them twice:

- **Before posting**: run all five on the storyboard and caption (the pre-flight in SKILL.md step 3b). Fix anything that fails before rendering.
- **After posting**: when the user shares numbers, find the weakest metric (against their own earlier reels in `marketing/reels/LOG.md`, not internet benchmarks) and run that lens in depth.

Each lens: take the role, fill in the specifics (platform, topic, audience, what the reel actually said), and give the user the asked number of concrete options, not general advice. Every option must be something you could put in the next reel's storyboard or caption.

The audience is always specific: e.g. "first- and second-year university students with 4–6 courses, mid-term or finals season, who already use Canvas or Brightspace and a notes app". Name the season and the moment.

---

## 1. Low views → the hook (Viewed vs. Swiped Away, 3-second hold)

**Role:** a viral content strategist for the platform, specialising in high-retention short-form video.

**Task:** the hook for a reel about "[topic]" isn't stopping the scroll. Write **5 hook variations** for [audience], each **under 2 seconds / 7 words on screen**, using proven triggers: emotion, surprise, or a mild controversy.

**Checks for each hook:**
- Frame 0 shows the problem (visual), the words say the same thing (text), and something moves.
- It names the student's situation, not the feature.
- It opens a loop the next beat must close (lean-in → "but" → snapback).
- The right people self-select in within 1 second ("3 exams" filters to students instantly).

**Output:** 5 hooks, each tagged with its trigger and formula, plus which 2–3 to render as A/B variants.

## 2. Low likes → relevance and emotional fit (likes per reach)

People watched but didn't feel anything. Likes mean "this is for me" or "this is good".

**Role:** a content relevance expert for the platform, skilled in analysing why posts underperform.

**Task:** the reel about "[topic]" gets low likes, so it isn't resonating with [audience]. Find **3–5 ways** to make the message, tone or angle more relevant and emotionally aligned with their interests, pain points or aspirations.

**Look at:**
- Is the pain specific and current (this week's exams, this term's Canvas mess) or generic ("stay organised")?
- Is there an aspiration, not just relief? (Calm, a free weekend, walking into the exam ready.)
- Does the payoff feel satisfying to watch (colour, motion, the moment the mess resolves)?
- Does it sound like a student or like a brand?

**Output:** 3–5 changes, each tied to a specific beat or line.

## 3. No comments → conversation triggers (comments)

People need a reason and an easy way to reply.

**Role:** a social media engagement strategist specialising in community building and comment-driven content.

**Task:** reels about "[theme]" get few comments from [audience]. Suggest **5 content tweaks or hook/comment-call strategies** that spark conversation, encourage replies, or provoke opinions. Use emotional triggers, open-ended questions, or debatable angles.

**Good comment triggers for Studyboard (calm brand, so debatable, never rage bait):**
- A question with a quick personal answer: "Which week is your hell week?" "What's your worst class this term?"
- A mild, defensible take students will argue with: "Paper planners don't work past 4 courses." "Studying the night before is a planning problem, not a discipline problem."
- A fill-in or choice: "Week 9, 10 or 11: which one's yours?"
- A deliberate open loop: name a thing and ask them to guess ("One of these exams is in-person. Guess which?").
- An on-screen prompt in the last second that matches the caption's question.

Avoid "Comment X for the link" bait (platforms demote it), and anything that mocks students or teachers.

**Output:** 5 tweaks, each marked as on-screen, caption, or pinned comment.

## 4. Low shares → "that's me" (sends per reach)

Sends are what reach new students, so they matter most for growth. People send things that say something about them or their friends.

**Role:** a viral marketing expert who specialises in highly relatable, share-worthy content.

**Task:** reels about "[idea]" get low shares, probably because they aren't hitting a relatable nerve with [audience]. Give **3–5 angles, storytelling techniques or punchy statements** that resonate emotionally, create "that's me" moments, or make people want to share.

**Angles that get sent:**
- **Tag-the-friend archetypes:** "the friend who does everything the night before", "the group project person", "the one with 6 courses".
- **Shared-moment specifics:** "11:58 PM Canvas refresh", "week 11 of every term", "the syllabus you never opened".
- **Useful-now:** something they'd forward because it helps the group right now (exam season, the first week of term).
- **Identity:** "If you colour-code everything, this is for you."

**Output:** 3–5 angles, each with the line or beat that carries it and who would send it to whom.

## 5. No followers → the profile (followers from reels, profile visits)

The reel worked, but the profile didn't convince them to follow.

**Role:** a growth strategist and profile optimisation expert for the platform, specialising in turning casual viewers into followers.

**Task:** the account isn't gaining followers, perhaps because the profile doesn't clearly show its value to [audience]. Niche: "a calm student planner: deadlines, exam prep, flashcards". Review the profile (**ask the user for screenshots** of the profile page, bio and highlights if you don't have them) and give **3–5 ways** to improve the bio, profile image, highlights, pinned posts and content positioning for clarity, credibility and follow-worthiness.

**First, check the scale.** With tiny reach (under ~1,000 views a month), the profile isn't the bottleneck, because almost nobody is reaching it. Say so, and put distribution first: posting cadence, series, Trial Reels, and the brand engaging in the niche (useful comments on student creators and campus accounts). Then do the profile fixes so the visitors who do come convert. The first review is in `marketing/instagram/profile-review-2026-11.md`.

**Look at:**
- Is the search keyword in the name field? ("Studyboard · Student Planner": Instagram search matches the name, not just the username.)
- Does the bio say who it's for and what they get, in one line? ("Plan your whole semester on one board. Free.")
- Do the pinned reels and the first 9 grid posts show a consistent series a student would want more of ("Exam season survival" series, "Week 11" series)?
- Is there a reason to follow, not just watch: a recurring format, a series name, "part 2" hooks?
- Link in bio goes straight to studyboardapp.com.

**Output:** 3–5 concrete changes, with suggested bio text.

## Pre-flight checklist (one line per lens)

Before rendering, write this in `brief.md` with a yes/no and a fix for each "no":

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| Views | Does frame 0 show the problem, with ≤ 7 words and motion, and open a loop? |
| Likes | Is the pain specific to this moment of the term, and is the payoff satisfying to watch? |
| Comments | Is there one easy, personal question or a mild debatable take, on screen and in the caption? |
| Shares | Who would send this to whom, and which line makes them do it? |
| Follows | Does this reel fit a series or recurring format, and does the profile promise more of it? |
