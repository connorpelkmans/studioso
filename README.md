# Studyboard

A calm student planner for courses, deadlines, study sessions and flashcards. It works as a website you can install on a phone (a PWA) and as a desktop app for Windows, Mac and Linux. No account is needed: your data stays on your device. An optional free account syncs it between devices.

## Features

- **Board and Today's Plan:** every task and deadline in one place, with a ranked plan for today that fits your study hours.
- **Essentials for new students:** a new install starts with just Board, Calendar, Review and Courses (the rest one tap away under More) and a calmer Today's Plan. Features turn on as they become relevant (a first deck, a first note, joining a group, an exam within 14 days), each announced once, and Settings > Appearance > Show Everything turns it off. Existing installs keep everything. Code can ask `SBESS.on()` and `SBESS.shows("groups")` (`src/js/01-essentials.js`).
- **Plan Ahead:** a Timeline and a Schedule (classes, exams and events, by week or month) in one tab. School Days holds days off, holidays, a rotating cycle and Term Dates: classes only show between the first and last class, so finals week and the break stay clear. Importing a calendar file (.ics) keeps repeating classes repeating, overnight events overnight, and can make breaks and holidays days off.
- **Exam location check:** an exam or quiz is only suggested as Do This Next (or given a focus slot) when it can be taken from home. Studyboard reads your school site (online quiz, paper, location), the instructions and your Schedule, uses AI only when those aren't clear, and asks you once with a one-tap question. In-person exams stay on the calendar and in Crunch, and their study sessions are still suggested. Your own answer (Where is this taken? on the exam) always wins.
- **Move it on the timeline:** drag a study block on Today's Plan's time strip to another time (long-press, then drag on a phone), or use its Move To… menu (times that fit, Tomorrow, Let Studyboard Pick). Classes, events and availability blocks stay put: a drop on one snaps to the nearest free time and says so. A moved block is pinned for that day and the rest of the plan goes around it; Undo reverses a move. Free, works offline.
- **Wrap Up Today:** in the last hour of your study day (or once today's plan is done), a quiet row at the bottom of Today's Plan opens a short wrap-up: what got done, Tomorrow / Pick a Day / Let It Go for each unfinished item, tomorrow's first task (it becomes Do This Next in the morning) and an optional "on my mind" note. One Undo reverses it; never a popup, not on a day off, and it can be turned off in Settings › Study Plan. Free, works offline.
- **Availability blocks:** add work, shifts and placements (including overnight and rotating rosters), travel time, other commitments and protected time. Today's Plan only schedules into the free time left and shows when, with your shifts on its time strip. Free.
- **Exam Prep Planner:** enter the exam date and topics and it spreads review sessions across the days before, pulling in the matching decks and notes. One sheet from every exam (row, detail sheet, Courses page, Today, the New exam form, a captured exam, Search Everywhere). Topics can be typed, pasted or suggested from your decks, notes, files and the chapters named in the exam notes; each gets Shaky / OK / Strong, an optional weight, and a list of linked material you can edit. Sessions follow spaced repetition (learn, then reviews at +1, +3 and +6 days, a recap in the last 48 hours, a timed practice a few days out, a light review the day before), fit your free time and avoid crunch days, and become ordinary Study tasks (one Undo removes them) that open the linked deck, note or quiz. Cards made from lectures and notes remember their date, so an exam pulls in the ones it covers (since the previous exam, or the dates, weeks or lectures its notes name) as topic material, sorted into topics by AI when it is set up. Your last exam reflection sets the defaults (learn new material sooner if you started late, topics start at Shaky if you felt more ready than your mark, more practice questions if they worked or your cards overestimated you), one line each with its own Undo. Short on time, it makes an honest crash plan and says what it dropped. A Prep plan view shows the day-by-day agenda, per-topic coverage and readiness (only once there is evidence), with Catch Up, Re-plan when the exam moves, and a wrap-up after the exam. Free and fully offline; AI, if set up, only words each session's instruction.

- **Exam Mode:** in the last 3 days before an exam or quiz, Today's Plan shows a small card with the countdown, readiness from the prep plan and a Study for It button; Do This Next, deck order (with an "Exam in 2 days" tag), Review, the companion's lines and the Focus timer's suggested task all point at that exam. Assignments are never buried: anything due sooner than your next study session for it (by the end of the exam day when none is planned), or short of time, comes ahead of exam study, and everything due in those days is listed on the card under Also Due. Not Now turns it off for one exam on this device; it ends on its own after the exam, and Settings has an on/off row. Free, works offline, no AI.
- **Crunch Forecast:** a third Plan Ahead view that colours every day and week of the term from calm to crunch, counting prep time before exams, papers and projects, so a week with three exams and a paper glows red weeks ahead. Tap a week for what is driving it, preview pulling work forward, and apply it with undo (school-synced tasks move their start date, not the due date). A card on Today and one gentle companion suggestion appear only for heavy weeks, once per week, and can be dismissed. Free, works offline, no AI.
- **Time Cushion:** a pill next to Time Today says how far ahead or behind you are over the next 14 days ("2h 30m to spare", "Tight: 20m spare by Tue", "45m short by Tue"), from Today's Plan's own numbers and your planning accuracy. Tap it for every deadline with its work and study time, the tightest marked, and a fix or two with Undo (add time today, study on a day off, start big work earlier, check estimates). Also in the widget feed. Free, works offline, no AI.
- **Big assignment breakdown:** one tap (or paste the prompt, drop a PDF, or tick a box when importing a syllabus) splits a paper, project, lab report or presentation into 3 to 8 milestones scheduled backward from the due date, on days with real free time and away from crunch days. Works offline from templates; AI only words the steps, never picks dates. One Undo reverses it all.
- **Quick capture:** add a task in seconds by typing a sentence (press C), sharing text or a link, a Shortcut or Siri link, or a photo of a whiteboard or slide with a due date. Clear sentences are added with Undo; anything unsure shows a one-tap check sheet. Everyday subject names find the course ("orgo" for Organic Chemistry, "english" for ENGL), the weight shows in the preview ("worth 10%"), and "next week" with no day is set to its Monday and checked before it's added. See SETUP-GUIDE, Quick Capture.
- **Repeats in quick add:** type a repeat in the add bar ("math homework every monday", "lab report every other thu", "reading weekdays until dec 12", "quiz every 2 weeks fri", "daily", "mon wed fri", "MWF", "TTh") and it makes the same repeating series as the New Task form's Repeat field, with the repeat words out of the title. The toast says the rule in plain words ("every Monday until Dec 15") with Edit and Undo (Undo takes the whole series away). Unclear phrases ("biweekly", "Everything Bagel essay") are left alone. Works offline, with or without AI.
- **Reading pace:** a reading with pages ("read ch 4 30 pages", "pp. 45-80", "pages 12 to 40", "30pg", or the Pages field in a task's Advanced Settings) shows "30 pages · about 1h 30m" and plans that time, at the course's reading pace (3 min a page to start) whenever you haven't set its hours yourself. Focus and logged time on finished readings fine-tune the pace (a smoothed average that sets odd ones aside), and a course page with readings has one Reading Pace field to change it.
- **Courses, grades and syllabi:** import a syllabus PDF (items already in your planner under your own names are spotted, and a breakdown line like "Research Paper 15%" weights that item), track grades (category weights like "Quizzes 20%" count in Today's Plan, Rough Week Rescue and the mark sheet too), "What do I need?", a Target Grade per course (Today's Plan gives more time to courses under it) and Rough Week Rescue.
- **Flashcards and quizzes**, notes with stickers, files, and Search Everywhere.
- **Math, chemistry and formatting:** notes, flashcards, quizzes, whiteboard text and the reader show `$x^2$`, `$$\int_0^1 x\,dx$$`, `\(...\)`, `\[...\]` and `\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}` as real formulas (KaTeX, loaded the first time one is shown), plus `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `~~struck~~`, `==highlight==` and `` `code` ``; on sticky notes, `# Title` and `## Heading` lines show as headings, and a formula wider than the note shrinks to fit. Text fields get a small toolbar with an equation editor (basics, calculus, Greek, symbols, chemistry and matrices, with a live preview; Alt+=, or Control+= on a Mac, opens it). Text stays plain, so it syncs, searches and exports as typed; Listen reads formulas as words. Free.
- **Word, PowerPoint and Excel files:** upload `.docx`, `.pptx` and `.xlsx` (and older formats) to Files and preview them in the app, page by page; AI can read them too. Pro opens them in the reader with highlights. An upload that hits a storage limit (storage full, a file too big, a type that can't be stored, or no storage) offers to keep it in your Google Drive or OneDrive instead, and saves it there once connected.
- **Class pages:** a course's Lectures tab lists every class of the term (lectures, labs, discussions, office hours), week by week and numbered (Lecture 8, Lab 4), with Term Dates you can set (until you do, the term is guessed to end the day before your first final exam). Each class has its own page with its notes and files (New Note, Upload, Google Drive, or pick from Studyboard) and its recording with the transcript and summary; older recordings are matched to the class they were made in. A class on the Schedule opens its page, and the course's Lecture Notes folder in Files shows the same things grouped by class. Uploads from a class go to <course>/Lectures/<date> <class> in Google Drive or OneDrive. Free.
- **Linked items:** a task shows one small Linked row with the notes attached to it, the notes that `[[link]]` it, its files, and the decks, quizzes and files its notes or Exam Prep plan link. Whiteboards can be `[[linked]]` by name, and Search Everywhere and Ask Your Course find them by their title and typed text. A deck shows the lecture, note or PDF its cards were made from (new cards record it) and the notes that link it. Nothing shows when nothing is linked. Free.
- **Document links on a task:** paste the Share link of a Google Doc, Sheet or Slides file, a Word, Excel or PowerPoint file, a OneNote page or a Canva design into a task's Documents section. Studyboard recognises the app (and the file name when the link carries one), and the task's view, its row on the Board and Today's Plan, and its Linked row open the document in one tap. Links only: nothing is fetched and no account is connected. Free.

Its pages come in as notes, stay up to date (your own edits in Studyboard are kept), and work with Ask Your Course, Make Cards and Exam Prep. **Save to OneNote** adds a note, a lecture's study notes or an Ask Your Course answer as a new page. Optional **Class Notebooks** add teachers' OneNote Class Notebooks from Microsoft Teams (read only). Needs a free account and `Notes.ReadWrite` on the Microsoft app (SETUP-GUIDE.md, OneNote). Free.
- **Share links and the Course Library:** share a deck, note or quiz as a link that opens in any browser with no account, and list a deck in its course's library so classmates at your school can find it (Flashcards > Course Library, or Decks From Classmates on a course page). Needs `supabase-sharing.sql`.
- **Public flashcards and quizzes:** publish a shared deck or quiz to its own searchable page at studyboardapp.com/flashcards or /quizzes, rendered on the server with flashcard structured data so search engines can list it (PUBLIC-PAGES.md). Explore in Flashcards and Quizzes opens them.
- **Match game:** on any deck, race the clock to pair 6 terms with their definitions (a wrong pair adds 1 second). Your best and recent times stay on the deck; in a study group you can post your time on a shared deck, with your name or anonymously, and see the group's best times. It never changes your review schedule.
- **Learn:** on a deck with 4 or more cards, Learn asks the cards in rounds of 7 until you know them all: first pick the answer from four (the wrong ones are other answers from the deck), then type it. A miss comes back later in the round, small typos are forgiven, and "I Was Right" counts an answer you meant. Math shows as math; picture cards are multiple choice only. It leaves your review schedule alone unless you tick Count toward review, and a session you leave picks up where you left off on that device. Free.
- **Ask Your Course** (Search Everywhere > Ask With AI, or Ask About This Course on a course page): ask a question about one course or all of them (it picks the course whose code you type). Answers come only from your notes, decks, files (with the PDF page), lecture transcripts and tasks, with numbered sources [1] [2] that open the exact item. When your material doesn't cover it, it says so, and Answer From General Knowledge is a separate, labelled answer that sends only the question. Follow-up questions keep the conversation, with 2 or 3 suggested next questions. Under an answer: Make Flashcards and Save as Note (with `[[links]]` to the sources), free with a key, and Quiz Me (Pro), a one-question-at-a-time tutor that checks your answer and explains it with its source. Only the best 8 passages (up to 1,500 characters each) are sent.

Each task gets the minutes spent on it: switching Working On (or starting a focus on another task) part way logs the time so far on the first task, and a session that ends while Studyboard is closed still counts, on the day it ended (after more than 3 hours, with a note instead of the alarm). Finished sessions fill the week's Focus Garden shelf in course colours (Focus sheet and Insights). ## Run it

**Website.** `index.html` is the app page; it loads the app's code and styles from `bundle/` (see *Where the code lives* below). Serve the folder over HTTPS (GitHub Pages works) or run a local server:

```
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

then open http://localhost:8080. The service worker (`sw.js`), `manifest.webmanifest` and the icons make it installable and available offline.

**Desktop app.** You need Node.js 20 or newer.

```
npm ci
npm run start        # prepares app/, widget/ and build/ from the files in this folder, then opens the app
npm run dist         # Windows installer in dist/   (also: dist:mac, dist:mac-intel, dist:linux)
```

`npm run prep` alone stages the offline copy of the page with its fonts and libraries. The `build-desktop` GitHub workflow (`.github/workflows/build-desktop.yml`) builds the installers when you push a tag such as `v1.13.0` or run it by hand from the Actions tab.

## Where the code lives

The app's code and styles are in `src/`, one file per section, so each part is easy to find and change:

- `src/js/NN-name.js`: the code, one file per feature (for example `src/js/80-groups.js`, `src/js/99-whiteboard.js`, `src/js/01-sync.js`). The `01-*.js` files are the app's core, by topic.
- `src/css/NN-name.css`: the styles.
- `src/app.json`: the order the files go in, and which are loaded only when needed.

The browser loads `bundle/app.js`, `bundle/app.css` and `bundle/scenes.js`, which are built from `src/`. **Don't edit `bundle/` by hand.** After changing anything in `src/`, run:

```
node scripts/csp.js
```

That one command rebuilds `bundle/`, updates the version stamps (`?v=`) in `index.html` so browsers and the offline copy pick up the change, and refreshes the page's script hashes. `npm test` fails while `bundle/` is out of date.

- **Same behavior as before.** The code files run together as one script inside the same wrapper as before, so they share everything exactly as when they were one file.
- **Scene library loaded only when needed.** The scene library (`src/js/20-scenes-lib.js`, about 3 MB) is the exception: it's `bundle/scenes.js`, loaded only for a scene theme or the Style Shop.
- **Searching:** to find where something is, search `src/` (for example `grep -rn "const SUPPORT" src/js`).
- **Error reports:** an error report's stack line such as `app.js:16336` maps back to its source file with `node scripts/app-files.js --where app.js:16336`.
- **One-page form:** `node scripts/app-files.js --bundle one-page.html` writes the whole app as one page, the way `index.html` used to be. `node scripts/app-split.js one-page.html` splits such a page back into `src/` (tools/insert-theme-batches.py works this way).

## Deploy

1. **Supabase (optional, for accounts and sync).** Create a free project and run the SQL files in the order in [SETUP-GUIDE.md](SETUP-GUIDE.md) ("Run the SQL files in this order"). Deploy the Edge Functions in `supabase-functions/<name>/index.ts` (calendar-feed, billing-webhook, lms-feed, send-reminders, delete-account and the others) as described there; `supabase/config.toml` holds each one's Verify JWT setting.
2. **Website.** On Cloudflare, `wrangler.jsonc` runs `scripts/build-site.js` on every deploy and serves the `site/` folder it builds (icons in `icons/`, widget files in `widgets/`, the website, and a strict script policy in `_headers`). For any other static host, run `npm run build:site` and upload `site/`.
3. **Desktop.** Tag a release, or run `npm run dist` locally.
4. **Before going public,** work through the *Public launch checklist* at the end of the setup guide and [RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md](RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md).
